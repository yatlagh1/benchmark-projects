EARLY MAX UNIT 4 WEEK 1 DAY 1 LESSON 2
Language and Literacy Collaborative Lesson
10 MIN

Collaborative Lesson

Standards

Handwriting (5 MIN.)

Introduce

We’ve been learning to write letters. Show the Sound-Spelling Card for Nn (nest). Point to uppercase N. Say: This is uppercase N. Today, we’ll learn to write uppercase N.

Model

Tell students that before they write, they will practice making uppercase N with their arms. Have students stand and echo your motions and words.

Use your whole body to make movements big, smooth, and playful. Move your body in the same direction as the writing motion so students can clearly see the direction and sequence of each letter-formation step.

Reach above your head with one hand, then lower it to the side of your body. Say: Touch, pull down.

Reach above your head with the same hand, then lower it in a diagonal motion. Say: Touch, slant right. Raise your hand straight up. Say: Push up.

Repeat 2–3 times.

Students with limited mobility can use hand and arm motions while seated.

Practice

Provide each student with a sensory bag. Have students use their finger to practice writing uppercase N on the bag. Prompt students to say /n/ each time they write the letter.

As students work, adults should circulate and engage with individuals and pairs of students to support letter formation and producing /n/.

Observe students as they write. Do students:

  • Produce the /n/ sound? (FLD.2.5)
  • Correctly form uppercase N? (FLD.4.2)

Use the Observation Record to monitor and assess students’ progress.

Foundational Skills: Introduce /n (4 MIN.)

Shuffle the previously taught Sound-Spelling Cards. Display cards one at a time.

Have students chorally respond to each question. Ask: What picture? What sound? What letter?

Direct students’ attention to the Sound-Spelling Card for Nn (nest).

  • Emphasize /n/ at the beginning of the word as you say: nnnest
  • Have students repeat the word. (nest)
  • Point to the letter Nn. Say: The letter Nn spells the sound /n/.
  • Have students chorally respond to each question. Ask:
    • What picture? (nest)
    • What sound? (/n/)
    • What letter? (Nn)
  • Play the Alphabet Video: “Letter Nn.”

Cross-Linguistic Connections: Explain the similarities and differences between /n/ in students’ home languages and English.

Differentiate Instruction

Support: Pair the sound /n/ with a gesture. Model pointing to your nose while stretching the sound /n/. Have students repeat the sound and gesture.

Challenge: Have students name words that begin with the /n/ sound.

Transition (1 MIN.)

Have students point to their noses and say /n/ as they transition to the Read-Aloud.

Resources

Workmats

  • Sensory bags (one per student)
  • Sound-Spelling Cards (Aa, Mm, Nn, Ss, Tt)
  • Alphabet Video: Letter Nn

Target Outcomes

Learning Outcomes

Language and Literacy

  • Understand and follow one-and two-step directions.
  • Name letters: Aa, Mm, Nn, Ss, Tt.
  • Recognize letter: N, n.
  • Produce sounds: /a/, /m/, /n/, /s/, /t/.

Physical Development

  • Coordinate movements across the body.
