Collaborative Lesson

Standards

Compare Objects by Size (9 MIN.)

Guide students in the Choral Counting Routine.

Show students a crayon. Say: When we notice something, we pay attention to it. We can tell others what we notice, like the way something looks.

Prompt students to describe the crayon’s color, shape, and size. As needed, model describing the crayon. Say: I notice the size of this crayon. It’s small.

Model how to make yourself small by squatting low to the floor. Say: I’m small.

Have students stand and copy your movement as they say: “I’m small.” Then, have students return to their seats.

Tell students to look around the classroom. Ask: What do you notice that is small? Have students Turn and Talk with a partner to name something small. Listen in and address any misconceptions. Have 2–3 students share their ideas with the group.

Show students a large stuffed animal. Prompt them to describe the color, shape, and size. As needed, model describing the stuffed animal. Say: I notice the size of this stuffed animal. It’s big.

Explain that big things take up more space than small things.

Model how to make yourself big by reaching your arms above your head as high as possible. Say: I’m big.

Have students stand and copy your movement as they say: “I’m big.” Explain that when they make their bodies big, they take up more space. Have students return to their seats.

Tell students to look around the classroom. Ask: What do you see that is big? Have students Turn and Talk with a partner to name something big. Listen in and address any misconceptions. Have 2–3 students share their ideas with the group.

Place the stuffed animal and the crayon side by side. Explain that you can compare the sizes of two things to see which is bigger or smaller.

Model comparing the stuffed animal to the crayon. Say: The stuffed animal is bigger than the crayon. It takes up more space than the crayon.

Prompt students to compare the crayon and the stuffed animal. Have students chorally respond to the question. Ask: Which is smaller, the crayon or the stuffed animal? (the crayon)

Ask: How do you know the crayon is smaller than the stuffed animal? Have students Turn and Talk with a partner. Listen in and address any misconceptions. Have 2–3 students share their ideas with the group.

Differentiate Instruction

Support: Provide students with two of the same objects in different sizes. Place the objects side by side and model how to compare their sizes.

Point to the bigger object. Say: This is bigger. It takes up more space.

Point to the smaller object. Say: This is smaller. It takes up less space.

Challenge: Have partners find two objects in the classroom. Ask: Which object is bigger? How do you know?

Observe students as they compare objects and discuss. Do students:

  • Recognize that objects can be compared? (Math.3.1)
  • Use words to describe sizes? (FLD.1.3)

Use the Observation Record to monitor and assess students’ progress.

Transition (1 MIN.)

Have students transition to the Read Aloud by taking big steps.

Resources

Stuffed animal and crayon

  • Numeral Number Cards (1–10)
  • large stuffed animal
  • crayon

Target Outcomes

Learning Outcomes

Math

  • Recognize numerals (1–10).
  • Recognize that objects can be compared.
