Language and Literacy Read-Aloud
15 MIN

Read-Aloud

Standards

Before Reading (7 MIN.)

Set Purpose

We're learning about communities. A community can be a place where people live or work, such as a city. Today, we’ll read a book called Good Morning, City.

Build Oral Vocabulary

Display the Oral Vocabulary Cards and use the 5-Step Vocabulary Routine on the back of each card to introduce the words: busy, peek, rush.

Build Vocabulary: Refer to the scaffolds on the back of the cards to support students as they make meaning of new language.

Introduce Good Morning, City

Display the front cover of Good Morning, City.

Point to the title and read it aloud. Point to the author and illustrator’s names and read them aloud.

Have students look at the illustration on the front cover and think about the title Good Morning, City.

Ask: What do you think this book is about? Have students Turn and Talk with a partner. Listen in as students discuss. Have 2–3 students share their ideas with the group.

Display the back cover of Good Morning, City. Explain that the illustrator made a picture on the back cover of the book.

Ask: What’s happening in the picture on the back cover? Have students Turn and Talk with a partner. Listen in as students discuss. Have 1–2 students share their thinking with the group.

Observe students as they write. Do students:

  • Use the story title to make predication? (FLD 3.2)
  • Describe illustrations on the back cover? (FLD 3.1; ELD 3.2)

Use the Observation Record to monitor and assess students’ progress.

During Reading (4 MIN.)

First Read-Aloud

Tell students you will read this book several times. Set a purpose for this reading. Say: As I read, listen to find out what happens in the city in the morning.

Read aloud Good Morning, City, pausing only to explain the meaning of drop-in vocabulary that may pose an obstacle for students. See the chart for suggested vocabulary and simple definitions.

After Reading (3 MIN.)

Ask: What happens in the city in the morning? Have students Turn and Talk with a partner.

Listen in and address any misconceptions. Have 1–2 students share their ideas with the group.

If students answer in 2–3 word phrases, remind them to speak in complete sentences. As needed, model student answers as complete sentences and

Extend the Conversation

What happens in the city in the morning?

Transition (1 MIN.)

Have students transition to Small Group or Centers by pretending to drive a delivery truck.
