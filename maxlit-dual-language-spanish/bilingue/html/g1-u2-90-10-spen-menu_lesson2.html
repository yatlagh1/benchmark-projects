Warm-Up: Review and Repetition

Set Purpose

As we continue to learn more about how different animals grow and survive, we will practice what we learned about reading and spelling words with the vowel sound /o/ at the beginning or middle of a word.

  • Display and read aloud the Student Learning Outcomes.

Cumulative Review: Read to Build Fluency (3 MIN.)

Display and have students go to "Big Cats" on page 2 in their My MAX Book. Have students whisper-read the text to cumulatively practice decoding previously taught skills and to build fluency.

Slide: "Big Cats"
  • Circulate and monitor accuracy of the last lesson's Quick Check activity.

Provide Corrective and Supportive Feedback: Circulate and listen in. For words students misread, say the word. Have students repeat, chorally trace and spell, then repeat again. Take note of missed sound-spellings for additional review.

Cumulative Review: Phonics and
High-Frequency Words (2 MIN.)

  • Have students carefully move to the carpet area with their My MAX Book, pretending to be squirrels looking for food.
  • Have students place their My MAX Book in front of them and direct their attention to the Phonics and High-Frequency Word Cards.
  • Use Phonics Cards 1-14 and High-Frequency Word Cards 10-34 and the Quick Cumulative Review Routine.
Slide: Quick Cumulative Review

Provide Corrective and Supportive Feedback: Circulate, listen-in, and note high-frequency words and phonics skills for which students need additional support. Include and highlight these in activities throughout the upcoming lessons.

Challenge students to use two high-frequency words in an oral or written sentence.

Explicit Instruction

Build Phonemic Awareness: Oral Blending (2 MIN.)

Model: lot

Listen as I say the sounds in a word: /l/ /o/ /t/. Listen as I blend, or string together, the sounds to say the word: /llllooot/, lot. The word is lot. What is the word?

  • To connect the sounds to print, display the word and say its sounds again, pointing to each letter as you make the sound: /l/ /o/ /t/.

Everyone, what's the word? (lot)

Practice: not, pot, pop, hot, hop, hops

/n/ /o/ /t/not
/p/ /o/ /t/pot
/p/ /o/ /p/pop
/h/ /o/ /t/hot
/h/ /o/ /p/hop
/h/ /o/ /p/ /s/hops
  • Repeat the Oral Blending Routine with the practice words.

For each word:

  • Say the sounds for each word.
  • Have students orally blend the sounds to pronounce the word.
  • Have students use print or letter tiles for support, as in the model above.

Support students with phoneme blending. See the Final Blending Routine. Show one letter at a time and say the sound, guiding students to slowly blend the word. For example: n /n/, n o /nnnooo/, n o t /nnnooot/.

Blend and Read (4 MIN.)

  • Display and have students go to Blend and Read on page 5 in their My MAX Book.
Slide: Blend and Read

Model

  • Use the Blending Lines Routine and model the first two words with /o/ spelled o.

Let’s look at the first word in line 1. Watch as I run my finger under the letters as I say and string together each sound: /tooop/, top. The word is top. Do it with me: /tooop/, top.

Now look at the next word. Watch as I run my finger under the letters as I say and string together each sound: /hooop/, hop. The word is hop. Do it with me: /hooop/, hop.

What do you hear that’s different in these two words? What do you see that’s different? (There’s a different sound at the beginning of each word. Top starts with t, while hop starts with h. Both words end with -op.)

Practice

Have students echo-read the rest of the words in line 1 after you. Then, chorally read line 1. Continue to have students echo-blend each line, followed by a choral read.

  • Lines 1–3 contain words with short o.
  • Lines 4–5 contain review words with previously taught skills to build fluency.

Provide opportunities for meaning-making using Vocabulary Boosters.

Direct students to read the challenge words on Line 6. This line contains challenge words with long o to differentiate the practice and accelerate learning.

Provide Corrective and Supportive Feedback: If students mispronounce a word, point to the missed sound-spelling, state it, have students repeat, then re-blend the word. Have students repeat. For example, point to the x in fox and say: The letter x stands for the /ks/ sounds. What sounds? Let’s blend this word together: /fffoooks/, fox.

High-Frequency Words (3 MIN.)

Introduce: for, have, jump, no, one

  • Display and have students go to Read and Spell It on page 6 in their My MAX Book.
Slide: High-Frequency Words
  • Play the High-Frequency Word Video for each word, then use the Read, Spell, Write, Apply Routine to introduce high-frequency words.
  • Have students write each word in their My MAX Book as they spell it aloud.

Patterns to Point Out

  • For for, point out that this is not the number four.
  • For have, point out that no English words end with the letter v. When a word ends in /v/, we must add a silent e.
  • For one, connect the word to the numeral 1.

Remind students that a filled-in red heart above the letter means it’s an irregular spelling and needs to be “remembered by heart.” An empty red heart shows a spelling that they will learn later but need to remember by heart for now.

Make Meaning: Provide opportunities for meaning-making using Gestures, Visuals, Home Languages, and Sentence-Level Context.

Develop Oral Language and Vocabulary

Build Vocabulary: family, seed, need (4 MIN.)

Display and have students go to Science Words to Know on page 5 in their My MAX Book.

Slide: Science Words to Know

Introduce the vocabulary words using the Words to Know Vocabulary Cards and the Read/Spell/Connect Routine. hand signals to show if the sentence is correct or if it needs Students will read these words in the texts this week.

Read: Read each word, and have students repeat. Model blending each word.

Spell: Chorally spell the word. Share unknown spellings, which students will not be held accountable for, such as the -eed spelling pattern in seed/need.

Connect: Use a picture or total physical response to discuss each word’s meaning, such as acting out being a “seed” by crouching down to be as small as possible, then standing and stretching arms up like a tree.

Add these words to the Knowledge Board. Prompt students to use the words when talking and writing about the unit selections by referencing the Knowledge Board frequently.
