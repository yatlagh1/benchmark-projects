Set Purpose

Today, we’re going to revisit “A Super Sense of Smell” to dig deeper into how foxes use their senses to explore and interact with the world.

  • Display and read aloud the Student Learning Outcomes.

Expand Content Knowledge:
Play Sensory Guessing Game (8 MIN.)

We’ll start with a short hands-on activity using our own senses.

  • Have students form pairs and determine Partner A and Partner B.

Each student will receive a bag with an item inside. We will begin with Partner A looking inside (secretly) and using their sense of sight to silently identify their object. Then, Partner B will try to identify the object using sound, smell, and touch.

  • Point out that using one's sense of taste could be unsafe.
  • Distribute bags and direct Partner A to look inside.
  • Have Partner B use two senses—sound, smell, or touch—to identify the item in the bag. Partner B should then write down their guess.
  • Repeat the process so that Partner B looks in their bag and Partner A uses sound, smell, and touch to identify what is in Partner B's bag.
  • Refocus the class and call on students from different pairs to answer:

Why was it helpful to use more than one sense in understanding what was in your bag? (Sample response: Each sense helps you gather information and interact with an item in a different way. Using more than one sense provides more information and allows a better guess at what is in the bag.)

Oral Expression: Read aloud the Guiding Questions to support students’ oral expression.

Deepen Understanding (10 MIN.)

  • Display and have students go to Apprentice Text 1, “A Super Sense of Smell” in their MAX Books (pages 6–7).
Slide: Apprentice Text 1

Now, we'll re-read “A Super Sense of Smell” and answer questions to gain a deeper understanding of how smell is a sensory superpower for canines. Be ready to share and explain your thinking.

  • Display and read aloud the Text-Dependent Questions.
Slide: Text-Dependent Questions
  • Have students work in pairs to complete each question. You may also have students count off by four and assign them the question that corresponds to their number.
  • Remind students to use the Annotate to Build Knowledge Routine as they work. count off by four and assign them the question that corresponds to their number.

Ask Guiding Questions to support students as they annotate text.

  • Monitor student discussions to determine their understandings using the Listen and Look Fors.

Strategic Grouping: Form pairs or groups of students at various levels of English language proficiency so that those needing more support can benefit from the help of their peers. See additional Grouping Strategies.

Discuss Answers and Explain Thinking (10 MIN.)

  • Call on a few students to share out their answers with the whole class.

Explain your thinking about how you arrived at your answer.

  • Direct other students to participate in the discussion using the Agree, Disagree, Add More Protocol.
  • If needed, refer to the Sample Modeling provided.

Question 1: What does “sensory superpower” mean?

Answer: The phrase “sensory superpower” refers to when one of an animal's senses is much stronger and more useful than its others, it can help the animal do extraordinary things.

Evidence: paragraphs 1 and 2, paragraph 2 photo and caption. If needed, refer to Question 1: Sample Modeling.

Question 2: Based on the text, how do canines collect information about their surroundings?

Answer: Canines use all five of their senses, but, in particular, they often rely on smell as a “sensory superpower” to gather information about their surroundings.

Evidence: paragraph 3, sentences 3–5. If needed, refer to Question 2: Sample Modeling.

Question 3: Using details from the text, explain three ways a fox's sense of smell can help it to find food.

Answer: A fox‘s sense of smell helps it find food that is a far distance away, under the ground, or left behind by humans.

Evidence: paragraph 1, sentences 3–4. If needed, refer to Question 3: Sample Modeling.

Question 4: How does a fox's sense of smell help to protect it?

Answer: A fox’s sense of smell can help it avoid danger and protect itself because it helps the fox sense if it is in another fox’s territory, if other animals are unfriendly, and if predators, like wolves, are near.

Evidence: paragraphs 2 and 4. If needed, refer to Question 4: Sample Modeling.

Activate Prior Knowledge and challenge students to make connections.

Wrap Up (2 MIN.)

  • Display and read aloud the Wrap Up.
Slide: Wrap Up Task

  • If time allows, call on a few students to share out with the class.

Plan for personalized instruction and independent activities as time allows.
